What the ladders show

Each card lists the next five prices a maker may post above the chosen price. Linear steps by the same amount everywhere. The two digit grids step by one unit of the fifth digit, which means their ladders match linear's near the bottom of a decade and coarsen on the way up. Geometric's prices never end in a round number: every one of them would have to be rounded to atoms before it could be multiplied by a quantity, which is why PageBook's matching path rules it out.

At 0.158 all four grids agree to within a rounding, because the example market's linear tick was chosen for that price. At 6.3 linear needs over six thousand keys while the others are unchanged.

The example market and the 200-write cap are PageBook's. The full comparison, including Hyperliquid's rule and the exchange tick tables the 1-2-5 grid comes from, is in the quantization schemes note.
